How the water works

Your walls, posts and leaves are saved in this browser as you go. Loading a map replaces them.

The current runs from clashing water toward water that agrees with a leaf's chord, faster where that changes more steeply, never faster than the waves travel. A leaf moves at half the current under it. Current scales how hard a slope pushes.

Real time runs the clock at a hundredth of true pitch: A4 vibrates 4.40 times a second and every note at its own true rate. A cell is then about 7 cm and the map an 11 m room.

Air loss grows with the square of the frequency, so highs fade over distance far faster than lows: B5 loses about 14 times what C4 does. Low notes also bend around walls more, straight out of their longer waves.

The water runs on your graphics card. It holds up to 16 voices: a post is one, each note of a leaf's chord is one.

Colour echoes splits every source's water by bounce. Its walls swallow the waves that hit them and hand the same reflection a hard wall makes to the next bounce, so direct waves cast true shadows and each echo can be seen on its own. Added back together they are exactly the real water. The map edge is not counted as a bounce.

With a hard edge and no air loss nothing ever leaves the map. Sound trapped behind hard walls rings on until air loss takes it.

Strength is how hard a source pushes the water, in dB: it changes how far it carries, how much it weighs in a clash and how it pulls on leaves, and you hear it too. The other Sound and colour settings change what you hear and see only; the clash maths and the leaves always use the field's own 8-overtone tone.
