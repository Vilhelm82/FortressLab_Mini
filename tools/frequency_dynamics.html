The current runs from clashing water toward water that agrees with a leaf's chord, faster where that changes more steeply, and never faster than the waves themselves travel. A leaf moves at half the current under it. Current scales how hard a slope pushes.

Air loss grows with the square of the frequency, so high notes fade over distance much faster than low ones: B5 loses about 14 times what C4 does. Low notes also bend around walls more, which comes straight out of their longer waves.

With a hard edge and no air loss nothing ever leaves the map, so the water keeps filling up. Open edges let it drain out of the world.
